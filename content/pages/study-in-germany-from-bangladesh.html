This page puts the whole route in order. Each stage below says what the official rule is, who sets it, and which guide explains it in detail. Where a figure changes from year to year, we do not copy it: we link the page that publishes it.

1. Eligibility: check your degree and your grade

German universities check your previous degree before they look at anything else. The anabin database of the German education ministers shows whether your institution in Bangladesh is a state or state-recognised institution (status H+), or whether additional criteria apply (H+/-).

Your grade is then converted to the German scale. German bodies use the modified Bavarian formula for this, but the conversion made by your university or by uni-assist is the one that counts.

[aq_stage_guides stage="eligibility"]

2. Applications: apply to programmes

Each university sets its own deadline for each programme, and the deadlines differ between semesters and applicant groups. Some universities ask international applicants to apply first through uni-assist, which issues a VPD for that university.

[aq_deadline topic="vpd-8-weeks"] [aq_stage_guides stage="applications"]

3. Offer: accept your admission

An admission letter is more than good news: at the German Embassy Dhaka, you need it before you can register for a student visa.

[aq_deadline topic="admission-letter"]

4. Money: prove your finances

For the visa you must show how you will pay for your living costs. The embassy lists three ways: a scholarship confirmation, a formal sponsorship declaration from a sponsor living in Germany (Verpflichtungserklärung), or a blocked account in Germany in your name. A scholarship can reduce the amount you must deposit.

[aq_volatile topic="blocked-account"] [aq_stage_guides stage="money"]

5. Visa: apply at the German Embassy Dhaka

New student visa applicants register in the Consular Services Portal. Master's applicants then receive an email with a link to book an appointment at VFS, where they hand in documents and give biometrics. Bachelor's applicants submit at the embassy itself. The embassy's old waiting list is closed to new entries; people already on it stay on it.

The decision is always made by the embassy's staff. VFS is not part of the decision.

[aq_deadline topic="visa-3-months"] [aq_volatile topic="visa-wait"] [aq_stage_guides stage="visa"]

6. Arrival: your first weeks

We have not published a guide for this stage yet. After arrival, your university's international office is your first official contact for enrolment.

What you can do yourself

You can apply on your own. The German Embassy Dhaka strongly encourages visa applicants to complete their applications themselves, and it warns about agents who charge high fees, make false promises or cancel appointments without telling their clients.

  • The embassy's study visa page and its student visa checklist are the official rules.
  • The DAAD Information Point in Dhaka, at the Goethe-Institut, answers questions about studying in Germany.
  • DAAD warns about agencies that falsely claim to represent German universities, and uni-assist states that it does not work with commercial agencies.
  • The DAAD database of international programmes lets you search programmes taught in English.
