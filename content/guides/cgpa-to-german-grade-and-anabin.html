The German grade scale

In Germany, 1.0 is the best grade and 4.0 is the lowest passing grade. A foreign grade is placed on this scale with a formula.

The formula

The education ministers' agreement on foreign grades calls it the modified Bavarian formula:

German grade = 1 + 3 × (Nmax − Nd) ÷ (Nmax − Nmin)

  • Nmax: the best possible grade in your grading system
  • Nmin: the lowest passing grade in your grading system
  • Nd: your own grade

The agreement takes Nmax and Nmin from the evaluations of the Central Office for Foreign Education (ZAB), which publishes them in anabin. The result is given to one decimal place and is not rounded.

A worked example

Illustration, not your result. Imagine a scale where the best grade is 4.00 and the lowest pass is 2.00, and a student has 3.00. Then: 1 + 3 × (4.00 − 3.00) ÷ (4.00 − 2.00) = 1 + 3 × 0.5 = 2.5.

Your own Nmax and Nmin depend on your institution's grading system as rated by the ZAB. Do not reuse the numbers above.

Whose conversion counts

The university's conversion, or uni-assist's rating inside a VPD, is the one that counts. TUM, for example, offers a converter on its website and states that the result is non-binding and that the examination regulations of each degree programme govern the official conversion.

How to check your university in anabin

  1. Open anabin's institution search guide and go to Institutionen.
  2. Choose the tab for searching institutions and select Bangladesh.
  3. Type part of your university's name and open its record.
  4. Read the status: H+ means a state or state-recognised institution; H+/- means additional criteria apply. Click the status to see its details.

anabin is partly in German only, and it is not complete. If your institution is missing, ask the university you are applying to.
