Who registers where

New applicants register in the Consular Services Portal (CSP). Only applicants with a valid admission letter can register there. You register yourself, with your own login.

Master's or bachelor's: two routes

Where you submit, by degree
You are applying forWhat happens after registration
A master's programmeThe embassy emails you a link to book an appointment at VFS. You cannot book without that email. You hand in your documents and give biometrics at VFS, which forwards your application to the embassy.
A bachelor's programmeYou send a confirmation email with your documents, and the embassy gives you an appointment. You hand in your documents and give biometrics at the embassy.

VFS is not part of the decision and cannot influence it. Every application is decided by the embassy's staff.

The old waiting list

The embassy's old appointment waiting list is closed to new entries but not deleted. If you are already on it, you do not register again in the new system.

The 3-month rule

[aq_deadline topic="visa-3-months"]

If your arrival is further away, the embassy asks you not to apply yet. It also states that enrolment and examination dates cannot be considered, so plan with your university if your visa may come late.

Priority for qualified students

Some applicants count as "qualified students" and may be prioritised: for example certain PhD applicants, holders of scholarships funded by the German government or by EU funds, and graduates of German schools. Eligibility is checked through a questionnaire in the CSP. A tuition waiver alone does not count, even if a university calls it a scholarship.

The appointment day

  • Arrive on time: if you are more than 15 minutes late, a new appointment is scheduled.
  • Bring the visa fee in cash, in taka. Cards and online banking are not accepted. The fee is fixed in euro and is not refunded if the visa is refused.
  • Keep your collection slip. It has a barcode number that you quote together with your passport number, and it is your payment receipt.
  • If a document is missing, the embassy explains how to send it through its webform.

Apply on your own, or with help

The embassy strongly encourages applicants to apply on their own: it saves time and money and keeps you in control of your data. It warns about agents who charge exorbitant fees, make false promises, cancel appointments without telling their clients or misuse identities, and it states that no agency can guarantee a visa.
